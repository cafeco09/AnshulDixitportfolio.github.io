Ann-On-Ally · Research

Research in progress.

The Lab explores how evidence, behaviour and interacting systems shape real decisions. These six projects ask different questions, from AI-supported judgement to public data and environmental risk.

Each entry is presented at its current stage: a simulation, an exploratory analysis, an evidence dashboard or a prototype. The research question leads; the project is the way I investigate it.

Six research threads

Questions first. Tools second.

These projects do not all make the same kind of claim. Some test a modelled mechanism; others organise observational evidence or demonstrate a decision-support idea. Their scope and limits are stated alongside each one.

Research 01Simulation study

Explanation, autonomy and AI advice

Can an explanation help preserve a person’s sense of autonomy when AI advice conflicts with their judgement?

A Monte Carlo simulation explores how psychological reactance and explainability may shape trust, disagreement and decision outcomes when a person receives AI advice. The Explainability Buffer is tested as a design hypothesis.

Scope: this models possible behavioural responses; it is not a clinical or user study and does not establish how deployed AI will affect people.

Research 02Exploratory analysis

Food patterns and fertility in Japan

What do prefectural fertility patterns and food-supply measures show, and what questions should be tested next?

This analysis brings together Japanese fertility, income, eating-out and food-quantity data, comparing regional patterns and exploring possible future scenarios. It separates historical observations from scenario assumptions.

Scope: the results are observational and sensitive to data coverage and specification. They do not establish that food imports or diet cause fertility changes.

Research 03Evidence dashboard

Girls’ education, in context

How do education measures, learning outcomes and social context vary across countries?

A 28-country evidence dashboard brings together indicators on participation, completion, foundational learning and wider context. Source periods, definitions and coverage gaps remain visible; SIYA supports bounded questions about the evidence.

Scope: measures come from different sources and years. Family-background comparisons are available only for covered locations and describe associations, not causal effects.

Research 04Public-data study

Food waste, hunger and access

Where do household food-waste estimates coexist with undernourishment, and what structural questions does that raise?

The dashboard compares country-level indicators for household food waste, undernourishment and malnutrition mortality. It frames overlap as a question about access, affordability, storage, distribution and resilience.

Scope: the indicators differ in source, period and certainty. Their overlap is descriptive; it does not show that household waste directly causes hunger or how much food could be redistributed.

Research 05Decision simulation

Demand forecasts and retail choices

How do recommended price and promotion actions change when a demand forecast is paired with practical constraints?

A retail decision prototype forecasts next-week demand, simulates candidate prices and promotions, and compares a revenue-only policy with one that penalises discount depth and promotion use.

Scope: the policies illustrate trade-offs in a predictive simulation. Observational promotion data cannot establish causal uplift, and penalty settings affect recommendations.

Research 06Visual prototype

Space weather and satellite drag risk

How might changes in space weather and atmospheric density be represented as orbital-drag risk for low-Earth-orbit satellites?

An interactive globe combines a 42-satellite sample with geomagnetic and atmospheric indicators, drag-risk categories and a selected-satellite view to make the relationship easier to inspect.

Scope: this is a risk-visualisation prototype, not an operational forecasting or satellite-safety system.
