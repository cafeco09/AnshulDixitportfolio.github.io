Decisions are systems

Bring the whole decision pathway into view.

A useful decision rarely comes from a model, dashboard or dataset alone.

It depends on whether the right evidence is available, how that evidence is interpreted, how people and intelligent systems respond to it, how information moves between them, and what happens after a decision is made.

Ann-On-Ally brings those parts together. I use Human-Centred Decision Intelligence to study and design the complete decision pathway.

Evidence→Reasoning→Behaviour→Decision→Coordination→Learning

The aim is not simply to produce an answer. It is to create decision systems that can explain what they know, recognise what they do not know, support meaningful action and improve through outcomes.

Six parts of a decision

Each part asks a different question.

Together, these six parts help reveal how evidence becomes action, where judgement enters and how a decision can improve.

01 / EVIDENCE

Evidence

What do we actually know?

Identify the information available, its provenance, limitations, conflicts and uncertainty.

02 / REASONING

Reasoning

What can legitimately be inferred?

Separate observation from interpretation and determine what the available evidence actually supports.

03 / BEHAVIOUR

Behaviour

How will people and intelligent systems respond?

Understand incentives, attention, trust, reactance, habits and interaction effects.

04 / DECISION

Decision

What should happen next?

Translate evidence and reasoning into choices that are useful, proportionate and actionable.

05 / COORDINATION

Coordination

How should the parts work together?

Examine hand-offs between people, models, data, tools, organisations and other systems.

06 / LEARNING

Learning

What happens after the outcome?

Use results, feedback and new evidence to improve the next decision.

What I work on

From decision questions to useful systems.

I work across evidence, reasoning, behaviour and systems design, adapting the approach to the decision problem.

01 / FRAMEWORK

Decision Intelligence

I investigate complex decision problems and identify where evidence, reasoning or decision processes can be improved. This can include defining the decision itself, identifying useful signals, evaluating uncertainty and designing a clearer path from information to action.

02 / AI SYSTEMS

AI Reliability & Reasoning

I examine how AI systems reach conclusions, what evidence supports those conclusions, where uncertainty should remain visible and when a system should abstain rather than overstate what it knows.

03 / PEOPLE & DATA

Behavioural Data

I combine behavioural science with quantitative analysis to understand how people respond to choices, information, interfaces and intelligent systems.

04 / CONNECTIONS

Systems Interaction

I study what happens between systems: how context is transferred, where information is lost, where responsibilities become unclear and where decision processes develop unnecessary friction.

05 / PROTOTYPING

Decision Prototypes

Where the problem is sufficiently defined, I build focused prototypes that demonstrate how evidence, reasoning and decision support can work together.

The interesting part is often between the components

The connections can determine the outcome.

A model can work correctly while the wider decision system still fails. That is why I look beyond individual models and examine the relationships between evidence, people, decisions and systems.

The quality of those connections often determines the quality of the final outcome.

  • A useful signal can be lost during a hand-off.
  • A confident output can hide weak evidence.
  • Two individually effective systems can interact badly.
  • A technically correct recommendation can be rejected by the person receiving it.
